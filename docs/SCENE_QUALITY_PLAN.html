  1. Art direction: believable contemporary Indian locations, restrained mystery atmosphere, realistic human proportions, warm practical lights against cooler night ambience. Keep dark teal, brass and Inter for interface elements. Avoid mixing realistic people with blocky principal characters.
  2. Free assets: acquire finished models with verified redistribution rights. Prefer Poly Haven CC0 and Microsoft Rocketbox MIT; use other original creator sources only after checking the individual license. Keep a source/license/modification record for each asset. No paid assets or homemade substitute hero models.
  3. Blender preparation: import source assets, inspect topology/materials, unify metres and axes, apply transforms, ground origins, measure floor/desk/seat surfaces, check normals and textures, make LODs and export optimized GLBs. Every scene consumes those values; no copied coordinate fixes. Replace floating yellow/blue rings with compact object-attached markers, group overlapping actions and use the same teal/brass controls throughout.

Current examples: Act 2 café narration showing the gazebo, workshop emergency with no emergency action, hospital discovery with an empty bed, and Act 7 mask dialogue without a physical mask. The spoken line must match the visible scene.
  6. Test harness: maintain a development-only gallery of all 14 environments and 62 distinct cinematic arrangements. It must use the actual game renderers, wait for drawing and animation settling, and avoid team progress writes. Keep before/after captures and a defect list.

3. Mandatory pass for each location

  1. Layout pass: floor and furniture contacts, scale, rotations, door/stair continuity, supported seated hips/feet, no floating props or intersections. Examine the actual animation cycle, not the bind pose.
  2. Art pass: compatible models, material maps, exposure, grounded shadows, believable density and clear composition. Remove conspicuous placeholders and accidental empty space; preserve deliberate walking/viewing space.
  3. Camera and narrative pass: audit all 109 cues, not just the 62 deduplicated arrangements. Correct room, time, cast, prop and action for each spoken line. Check wide, two-person and portrait views plus every opening, closing, replay and moving transition. Confirm feet remain in wide shots, faces remain unobstructed and dialogue controls stay inside the viewport. Check 1920×1080, 1366×768, 1280×800, 1280×720, portrait and short landscape layouts.
  4. Interaction pass: discover every clue, open every puzzle, inspect artwork, consult the notebook, replay dialogue and use every exit. Markers follow the object surface and remain readable. Test keyboard, pointer and touch fallback controls.
  5. Audio pass: listen to all recorded lines for pronunciation, intelligibility and role suitability. Regenerate weak takes. Verify pause/resume, mute, skip, replay, subtitle timing, mouth movement and music ducking. Test blocked autoplay, a failed audio download and returning to the same room.
  6. Performance and recovery pass: profile an integrated-GPU classroom device; balanced target at least 30 FPS and desktop high target 60 FPS. Record frame times, loading bytes/time and resource counts. Lazy-load rooms, use 1k/2k textures appropriately, limit active shadows and instance background props. Check memory after at least 20 transitions, cold cache, slow network, failed asset/audio, refresh and reconnect. Optimize based on measurements.
  7. Evidence: save representative before/after images, all-shot captures, contact measurements, performance observations and remaining defects. Mark the location complete only when the checks pass.

6. Vercel release gate

  1. Keep incremental production updates separate from the final-quality claim. Before calling the game finished, complete every environment and story phase with build, asset, staging, voice, engine, campaign and event checks; preserve source licenses and a rollback revision.
  2. For each update, deploy a versioned Vercel candidate and validate its API version, changed assets, entry restrictions and cold load before production promotion.
  3. The completion gate is zero known critical/major defects: no crash, progression blocker, missing narrated action, major clipping/intersection, unreadable clue or broken audio control. Record lesser defects and confirm they do not undermine the intended experience.
  4. After promotion, check team entry/resume, timing and leaderboard without using live participant data. Do not call the game finished while any location or phase remains in review or classroom performance remains untested.

Free speech implementation

Kokoro-82M has Apache-licensed weights. The implementation generates stock-voice recordings locally through Kokoro ONNX and serves MP3s. Clients do not download or run the inference model; no TTS API subscription is required. The catalog covers 227 unique authored lines (21.9 minutes, 12.7 MiB total), fetched as needed. Newly authored dialogue requires regeneration and listening review.
